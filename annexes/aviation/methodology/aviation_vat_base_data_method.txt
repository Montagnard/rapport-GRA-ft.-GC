METHOD: VAT BASE OF INTRA-EU AND NATIONAL AIR TRAVEL (aviation_vat_base_data.py)
==================================================================================

This note describes how aviation_vat_base_data.py estimates the VAT base of passenger
air travel within the EU-27 (national and intra-EU flights), splits it across the member
states, and expresses the revenue of two reform scenarios relative to each member
state's GNI.

All values are in EUR. "bn" = billion, "m" = million. "bp" = basis point (1 bp = 0.01%).

Columns:

    Base EUR bn  VAT base of national and intra-EU flights assigned to the country
    Share %      country base / EU-27 base x 100
    GNI %        country GNI / EU-27 GNI x 100
    Ratio        Share % / GNI %
    Reform bp    revenue of the passenger-flow-preserving reform collected in the
                 country, in bp of the country's GNI
    +20pp bp     additional revenue from a further additive VAT increase of 20
                 percentage points collected in the country, in bp of the country's GNI

A ratio above 1 means the country holds a larger share of the aviation VAT base than of
EU-27 GNI. Member states are sorted by the last column.


2. FORMULA
----------

    Base = flight legs x average revenue x taxable share of revenue

    flight legs     = national passengers + 0.5 x intra-EU passengers
    taxable share   = 1 - business share of revenue x share of VAT deducted


3. STEPS
--------

Step 1: Flight legs per member state: 516.6 m in total

- National and intra-EU passengers carried per member state are taken from Eurostat's
  air passenger transport statistics [1]. National passengers total 150.8 m, intra-EU
  passengers 731.5 m.
- National passengers are counted in full.
- Intra-EU passengers are counted at 50%, because each intra-EU flight is recorded both
  as a departure in one member state and as an arrival in another. Counting both would
  count each passenger twice. Each member state is therefore assigned half of its
  intra-EU passengers.

Step 2: Average revenue: EUR 98 per leg

- [ASSUMPTION] An average revenue (fares plus ancillary revenue) of EUR 98 per leg. It is anchored on the average revenue
  of Ryanair (EUR 70 per leg) [2] and Wizz Air (EUR 83 per leg) [3], which carry a large
  share of intra-EU passengers, and on the assumption that network carriers charge
  higher fares on point-to-point and feeder legs.
- Revenue: 516.6 m legs x EUR 98 = about EUR 50.6 bn.

Step 3: Business travel and VAT deduction: taxable share 77.7%

- Corporate travel accounts for 20-25% of air passengers [4]. The mean of 22.5% is
  used.
- [ASSUMPTION] Business travel costs 1.33 times as much as other travel, which is
  plausible given flexible fares and late booking. Business travel therefore accounts
  for 22.5% x 1.33 / (22.5% x 1.33 + 77.5%) = 27.9% of ticket revenue.
- [ASSUMPTION] 80% of the VAT on business travel is effectively deducted as input VAT,
  allowing for some existing exceptions.
- Taxable share of ticket revenue: 1 - 27.9% x 80% = 77.7%.
- VAT base: EUR 50.6 bn x 77.7% = EUR 39.3 bn.

Step 4: Share of GNI

- EU-27 GNI is the sum over the 27 member states (EUR 18,115 bn) [5].
- [ASSUMPTION] Bulgaria only reports Q1 2024, so its annual GNI is Q1 x 4.

Step 5: Reform revenue in basis points of GNI

- Revenue of each reform scenario is distributed across member states in proportion to
  their share of the VAT base (Share %) and divided by the country's GNI:

      bp = EU-27 revenue x Share % / country GNI x 10,000

- Passenger-flow-preserving reform: EUR 2.5 bn for the EU-27.
- Further additive VAT increase of 20 percentage points: an additional EUR 8 bn for
  the EU-27.
