METHOD: EU-27 LUXURY CONSUMPTION TAX BASE (luxury_good_data.py)
==================================================================

This note describes how luxury_good_data.py estimates the annual EU-27 tax base for a
luxury consumption tax and splits it across the 27 member states. It covers the two
output tables, the method used for each sector, and assumptions made along the way.

All values are in EUR. "bn" = billion, "m" = million.


1. OUTPUTS
----------

Table 1: EU-27 spending per sector (EUR bn per year).

    Luxury cars                       88
    Personal luxury goods             81
    Hospitality (hotels, cruises)     54
    Yachts                            25
    Fine wines and spirits            22
    Gourmet food and fine dining      16
    Design and furniture              11
    Total                            296

Table 2: the same base split across the 27 member states, sorted from highest to lowest
base. Columns:

    Base EUR bn  sum of the country's base over all seven sectors
    Rev. %       country base / EU-27 base x 100
    GDP %        country GDP / EU-27 GDP x 100
    Ratio        Rev. % / GDP %

A ratio above 1 means the country holds a larger share of the luxury base than of EU-27
GDP.


2. COMMON CONVERSION: EUROPE TO EU-27
-------------------------------------

Several sources report values for "Europe" rather than the EU-27. These are scaled down
with a single factor of 0.74 throughout.

[ASSUMPTION] The 0.74 factor originates from the personal luxury goods estimate: Bain/
Altagamma report EUR 110 bn for Europe, including the UK, Switzerland, Turkey and Norway
[1], which was reduced to about EUR 81 bn for the EU-27. The same factor is applied to
the other sectors, although the "Europe" definitions of the sources may differ (ECCIA
defines Europe as the EU-27 plus the UK and Switzerland [2]).


3. SECTORS BASED ON THE ECCIA/BAIN REPORT
-----------------------------------------

Sectors: personal luxury goods, hospitality, fine wines and spirits, gourmet food and
fine dining, design and furniture.

Method: EU-27 value = global market size [2] x share bought in Europe x 0.74

    Sector                          Global (EUR bn)   x 30%   x 0.74   Table 1
    Personal luxury goods                 363                            81
    Hospitality (hotels, cruises)         242                            54
    Fine wines and spirits                 99                            22
    Gourmet food and fine dining           72                            16
    Design and furniture                   51                            11

- Global market sizes are taken from ECCIA/Bain (2025), Table 4 [2].
- The 30% share bought in Europe is taken from ECCIA/Bain (2025), Diagram 3 [2].
  [ASSUMPTION] ECCIA does not make fully clear whether the 30% refers to all sectors or
  only to personal luxury goods. It is applied uniformly to all five sectors here.
- ECCIA measures market size at retail-equivalent value, net of VAT, for brands
  recognised as high-end or luxury by reputation, marketing standards and selective
  distribution [2].


4. LUXURY CARS
--------------

The tax is designed to apply only to the part of a car's price above a threshold. The
threshold is the average net price of a new car.

Method:

  1. Market volume = new passenger car registrations x average net price
     = 10,822,831 [3] x EUR 32,000 = about EUR 346 bn.
  2. Car prices are modelled as log-normally distributed, with a mean of EUR 32,000 and
     a standard deviation of the log price (sigma) of 0.65.
  3. The tax base is the expected value of max(price - 32,000, 0), i.e. only the excess
     of each car's price over the threshold. With setting the threshold equal to the mean 
     price, the excess is about 25.5% of the market volume, i.e. about EUR 88 bn.

Assumptions:
- [ASSUMPTION] Average net price of EUR 32,000 for the EU-27. For comparison, DAT
  reports an average purchase price of EUR 44,560 for private buyers in Germany in 2025
  (about EUR 37,400 net) [4], and UNRAE reports an average transaction price of EUR
  30,071 in Italy in 2024 (about EUR 24,600 net) [5].
- [ASSUMPTION] Log-normal price distribution with sigma = 0.65.
- [ASSUMPTION] The threshold equals the average net price. It is a policy choice and
  somewhat arbitrary.


5. YACHTS
---------

Yachts are valued as an annual flow of consumption:

    annual consumption per yacht = (r + delta) x K + OPEX,   with OPEX = o x K

    r      return on capital: 4% for all yachts [ASSUMPTION]
    delta  depreciation rate, by size class (see below) [ASSUMPTION]
    K      capital value of the yacht, by size class
    o      operating expenses as a share of K: 10% below 30 m and 15% above 30 m,
           based on Fraser Yachts' estimate of 10-15% of purchase value per year [6]

Yachts are counted as full-year-equivalent presence in EU waters, i.e. a yacht that
spends four months a year in EU waters counts as one third of a yacht.

5.1 Yachts over 30 m

- Global fleet: 6,174 yachts over 30 m in operation [7].
- Size distribution of the fleet [7]: 63% at 30-40 m, 21% at 40-50 m, 13% at 50-80 m,
  and 3% over 80 m.
- [ASSUMPTION] Effective time spent in EU waters: 32%.
- [ASSUMPTION] Capital values per yacht, loosely based on yacht broker listings that
  mix yachts of different ages [8, 9, 10]: 30-40 m EUR 15 m; 40-50 m EUR 27 m;
  50-80 m EUR 50 m; over 80 m EUR 150 m.
- [ASSUMPTION] Depreciation falls by one percentage point per size class, starting
  from 10% below 30 m: 30-40 m 9%; 40-50 m 8%; 50-80 m 7%; over 80 m 6%.

    Class       Yachts (EU, full-year equivalent)   K (EUR m)   r + delta + o   EUR bn/yr
    30-40 m                  1,245                     15            28%            5.2
    40-50 m                    415                     27            27%            3.0
    50-80 m                    257                     50            26%            3.3
    over 80 m                   59                    150            25%            2.2
    Total over 30 m                                                                13.8

    Yachts (EU, full-year equivalent) = 6,174 x fleet share x 32% time in EU waters.

5.2 Yachts of 10-30 m

No harmonised EU register of yachts exists, so the number of 10-30 m yachts is
approximated from marina berth capacity.

- Marina berths in the EU member states among Europe's top countries, from ICOMIA [11]:
  Sweden 500,000; France 253,000; Italy 181,000; Netherlands 165,000; Spain 130,900;
  Poland 48,900; Croatia 18,200; Greece 15,000; Portugal 13,300; Ireland 10,000.
- Share of berths occupied by 10-30 m yachts:
    Italy        28%  Il Diporto Nautico [12]
    Sweden        3%  Transportstyrelsen boat survey: share of boats over 10 m [13]
    France       28%  [ASSUMPTION]
    Spain        15%  [ASSUMPTION]
    Croatia      15%  [ASSUMPTION]
    Greece       15%  [ASSUMPTION]
    Portugal     15%  [ASSUMPTION]
    Netherlands   7%  [ASSUMPTION]
    Poland        7%  [ASSUMPTION]
    Ireland       7%  [ASSUMPTION]
- This gives about 178,800 yachts of 10-30 m. EU countries without berth data are not
  counted.
- [ASSUMPTION] Average capital value K = EUR 0.25 m, with delta = 10% and o = 10%.
- Annual consumption: 178,800 x EUR 0.25 m x (4% + 10% + 10%) = about EUR 10.7 bn.

5.3 Total

Yachts over 30 m (EUR 13.8 bn) plus 10-30 m (EUR 10.7 bn) = about EUR 25 bn per year.
The result is sensitive to the size threshold, the capital values and the 10-30 m yacht
count, all of which rest largely on assumptions.


6. SPLIT ACROSS MEMBER STATES (TABLE 2)
---------------------------------------

Each sector's EU-27 value from Table 1 is split across the member states with a
sector-specific allocation key:

    country base in sector = country key for that sector x EU-27 sector value

[ASSUMPTION] No data on national luxury consumption exist. The country keys are therefore 
constructed. They are mostly anchored on the following parameters, combined with assumed 
weights, to generate a key for each country and sector:

- GDP and population, (partly GNI-adjusted)
- New car registrations and premium-brand shares
- Tax-free shopping by tourists
- Luxury hotels and price levels for restaurants and hotels
- Marina berths and yacht charter activity
- Wine consumption
- MICHELIN-starred restaurants

For details, please contact Lennart Stern (lennarts@pik-potsdam.de).
